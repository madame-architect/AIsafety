## 1. A benchmark result describes performance under an evaluation configuration

The technical corpus contains capability tests, adversarial safety tests, training and mitigation experiments, evaluator studies, and proposed evaluation methods. They yield different evidence. Phuong et al. v2 reports several early-warning evaluations. Table 23 and Appendix F.5 concern a toy local safety-filter modification task in self-reasoning: the Pro-CoT easy/easy condition scores 0.50 against a 0.70 passing criterion. This is distinct from the self-proliferation tasks. Table 11 uses modeled one-sided 97.5% upper bounds for milestone-based tasks, conditional on the stated priors and milestone assumptions. These results characterize that protocol and model configuration. Folkerts et al. uses multi-step enterprise and industrial-control ranges with explicit inference budgets. OSWorld, TheAgentCompany and τ-bench expand the scope to tool use, longer workflows and repeated-trial reliability, respectively. Each adds a missing dimension. Any comparison requires matching model version, harness, tools and permissions, retries, time or tokens, safeguards, judge and task distribution. Even a real application can be part of a controlled environment. [Phuong v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793v2) · [Folkerts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214) · [OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972) · [TheAgentCompany](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161) · [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)

The differences can reverse apparent rankings. The RE-Bench authors report the best tested agents ahead at two hours and human experts ahead at longer aggregate budgets. The exact proceedings methods now confirm that the 32-hour human point is best of four eight-hour attempts, and experts could use LLMs and other tools. It compares autonomous agents with expert workflows under time budgets; it does not isolate the effect of adding AI to a human-only baseline. Murray et al.'s experts disagreed on whether strong CTF performance removed a practical bottleneck or left the harder task of connecting capabilities in a live workflow. Their elicited conditional probability change is expert judgment. WMDP evaluates hazardous knowledge and unlearning on a proxy, while Joshi et al. and Liu et al. examine leakage under changed prompts and the inadequacy of some unlearning metrics. Together these studies support explicit transfer tests and boundary conditions before translating proxy results into an operational threshold. [RE-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wijk25a.html) · [Murray v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04299v2) · [WMDP](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24bc.html) · [Joshi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15477) · [Liu](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-00985-0)

Cost-controlled and multi-metric studies add constructive methods to this configuration argument. Kapoor et al. show that simple retry baselines can change apparent architecture rankings and that joint cost–accuracy optimization can improve usable designs in selected benchmarks. HELM supplies common adaptation and an explicit scenario–metric coverage matrix, while documenting the subspace it leaves unmeasured. DecodingTrust finds both standard-condition improvements and weaknesses under altered access, prompts or fairness conditions. These sources support better comparisons and design choices within a stated purpose; they do not imply that every added metric is valid or that capability and safety have one universal relationship. [Cost and decision purpose](claims.md#c43) · [HELM](claims.md#c46) · [DecodingTrust](claims.md#c47).

METR’s time horizon adds a useful reliability measure: human expert task duration defines the difficulty scale, and the selected success probability defines the horizon. It measures neither how long an agent runs unattended nor whole-job automation; the current page’s domain, context, uncertainty and maintenance limits travel with it. Joint UK/U.S. o1 testing adds a concrete external evaluation with scaffold repairs, finite access, attempt/message budgets and version-specific caveats. The newly accessible technical report strengthens the methods trail without becoming a release-safety endorsement. [Time-horizon interpretation](claims.md#c78) · [Joint evaluation configuration](claims.md#c01).

Evidence trail: [configuration and task scope](claims.md#c01), [Phuong correction](claims.md#c02), [budget comparisons](claims.md#c03), [unlearning](claims.md#c04).

## 2. Safeguard results depend on the adversary, control and outcome definition

### Adversaries and tested interventions

Anil et al. found that several fine-tuning methods lowered baseline many-shot jailbreak success while leaving the growth in success with more examples largely intact. Ackerman and Panickssery report flatter slopes under their adversarial fine-tuning and input sanitization on Llama 3.1 8B, under the tested context limit. Some paired judgments also favored untuned answers on benign long conversations. Different models and training examples leave the disagreement about generality open. Sharma et al.'s Constitutional Classifiers and subsequent adaptive jailbreak studies use different defense generations, success criteria and feedback. Their results cannot be pooled as a single bypass rate. [Anil §§5.1–5.2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ea456e232efb72d261715e33ce25f208-Paper-Conference.pdf) · [Ackerman and Panickssery v3 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09604v3) · [Sharma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18837)

The security literature covers distinct control surfaces. Instruction hierarchy and prompt-injection defenses address conflicting instructions. Open-weight tamper resistance addresses model modification. Pretraining poisoning and backdoor studies concern different attacker access, objectives and persistence. A refusal rate cannot establish system authorization or egress enforcement, and a successful tamper-resistance result remains bounded by the tested modification budget. NIST's adversarial ML taxonomy provides useful language for comparing threats, and supplies no blanket assurance for the controls it describes. [Wallace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208) · [Tamirisa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) · [Souly on poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07192) · [NIST AI 100-2e2025](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-2e2025)

JailbreakBench and HarmBench also provide affirmative methodological and defense evidence. Artifacts, resource accounting, judge checks and matched benign behaviors make some comparisons more interpretable. The particular JBB defense tables use transferred nonadaptive attacks; those results bound worst-case resistance rather than establish it. HarmBench's adversarial training and Qi's recovery-data interventions improve tested endpoints, with attack-diversity, model-size and utility-comparator limits. JBB reports shared TDC/HarmBench behaviors, and GOAT uses JBB behaviors and judging, so their results cannot be counted as independent task-distribution corroboration. [Evaluation methods](claims.md#c33) · [Positive defense results](claims.md#c34) · [CyberSecEval2 scope and numerical hold](claims.md#c36).

Positive mitigation evidence extends beyond one defense family. SafeRLHF separates helpfulness and harmfulness in constrained training, with an expected learned-cost target. HC-RLHF adds a held-out accept-or-abstain test; under its statistical assumptions, the bound concerns the procedure returning a model that violates that cost constraint, rather than every response or operational harm. Its written nonzero-threshold centering and judge identity remain unresolved. Safe LoRA improves tested adaptation outcomes under trusted-weight and controlled-pipeline assumptions, with utility tradeoffs. Deliberative alignment and ReFAT add policy-reasoning and refusal-feature intervention/training results, with information, scorer, adaptivity and transfer limits. These are useful gains under specified conditions, without a cost-free or universal-safety conclusion. [Constrained training](claims.md#c92) · [Held-out confidence](claims.md#c93) · [Safe LoRA](claims.md#c94) · [Policy reasoning](claims.md#c95) · [ReFAT and other defenses](claims.md#c34).

AILuminate supplies another constructive instrument: versioned tests, explicit hazard/use rules, confidential evaluation and configuration-sensitive feedback. The current portal describes a different evaluator from the v1.0 paper’s LLM ensemble. Treating their versions as one fixed instrument would misstate what produced a score. Neither standardization nor a passing score settles deployment safety or acceptable risk. [Versioned benchmark evidence](claims.md#c79).

### Privacy and training persistence

Privacy introduces another outcome beyond knowledge-probe suppression. The inspected Hayes preprint distinguishes per-example from population membership-inference auditing and reports heterogeneous forgetting and worsened privacy for some retained examples. This corrects the inherited user-level shorthand and adds a collateral-effect question: which examples become more exposed after the intervention? The final SaTML edition remains unauthenticated; no final-paper full-read upgrade is inferred. [Per-example and retained-data privacy](claims.md#c35).

Training persistence depends on the threat model and objective. Sleeper Agents retains engineered conditional behavior under selected pipelines, alongside smaller-model or training-condition mitigation successes. Zhang’s data-only pretraining study finds persistence for triggered denial-of-service/context extraction and non-triggered belief manipulation, but no measured persistence for its tested jailbreak objective after safety training. Its shortened pretraining regime limits scale transfer. Cloud’s synthetic-data trait transfer and Gloaguen’s post-adaptation activation add different failure modes, with model-pair, task and configuration nulls. BackdoorLLM supplies structured access/endpoint distinctions and positive targeted-refusal mitigation; its later jailbreak task/baseline provenance remains unresolved. These are not one common persistence rate or evidence of naturally occurring deceptive intent. [Persistence and nulls](claims.md#c88) · [Adaptation boundary](claims.md#c89) · [Synthetic transfer](claims.md#c90) · [Benchmark/defense provenance](claims.md#c91).

Privacy and process verification introduce further distinctions. Nasr’s historical extraction results are conditional on model/interface access and web-matching ground truth. Du’s synthetic classification experiments separate single-model erasure from before/after update-history leakage, retaining exact retraining as a positive erasure control. vTune offers evidence of training on marked data without proving every requested model, iteration or method, or a fully calibrated tiny significance level. Barbero’s useful training-like data recovery remains distinct from exact membership or complete safety-policy recovery. [Extraction scope](claims.md#c96) · [Erasure versus update privacy](claims.md#c97) · [Process verification](claims.md#c98) · [Post-training data recovery](claims.md#c99).

The official [Pandora’s White-Box withdrawal notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17012v4) reports an experimental software bug. Its record is retained for history, with withdrawn status and an explicit non-support role; the unreliable archived numerical abstract has been removed from current exports. It supplies no empirical support here.

Evidence trail: [many-shot defenses](claims.md#c05), [classifier criteria](claims.md#c06), [threat/control surfaces](claims.md#c07). The supplied citation correction identifies Liu’s intended USENIX paper while retaining its existing key; StruQ is a separate work.

## 3. Evaluation integrity is itself an empirical question

### Feedback, reward models and judges

The alignment and oversight papers offer ways to improve preference data, reward learning, debate, and model grading. HelpSteer2 and its preference extension share data lineage. Better RewardBench performance can support a narrow judge comparison. Wang et al.'s positional-bias study and Xu et al.'s leakage study address distinct threats to relying on a judge or leaderboard. Xu's string matching is a proxy for possible exposure, and the printed score definition and threshold need reconciliation with the released code before using the rates. Khan et al. and Kenton et al. find debate can help under specific information asymmetries and judge setups. In Kenton's extractive QA experiment, judges with the full passage and direct QA outperformed debate across the tested extractive judge configurations. Kenton v2 also distinguishes overall accuracy from error detection: open consultancy can be more accurate overall while open debate better identifies protagonist errors. Khan v4 Appendix A.6 reports a quote-only ablation outperforming quotes plus arguments; evidence selection and verification remain an alternative explanation for some benefit. These are inference-time findings under the tested information access and judge setups, rather than proof of a general training method. The results favor validation against humans, held-out distributions and simpler controls for each intended use. [HelpSteer2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08673) · [HelpSteer2-Preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01257) · [Wang on judge bias](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/) · [Xu on leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18824) · [Khan v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06782v4) · [Kenton v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04622v2)

The broader oversight corpus includes training results. Burns tests whether weak labels elicit stronger performance; Lang combines debate-generated information with weak supervision, and Arnesen trains debaters with self-play. These provide affirmative results under their tested tasks and model gaps. Double or ensembled consultancy, training-data exposure, judge calibration and additional compute offer competing explanations for some gains; explicit refutation is not established as the sole mechanism. Sudhir's proposed incentive metric evaluates prompted true/false advocacy, a different target from observed learned honesty or judge accuracy. [Weak-to-strong transfer](claims.md#c28) · [Debate training](claims.md#c29) · [Accuracy versus incentive proxies](claims.md#c32).

Reward-model evaluation supplies a direct test of whether an attractive proxy predicts its intended use. RewardBench makes preference contrasts and refusal/response categories inspectable. PPE goes further by training one base policy with nine selected reward models and comparing downstream human preferences; some granular metrics are more predictive in that setting than familiar aggregate rankings. This is affirmative downstream validation with a limited model/optimizer sample, rather than a general reason to discard benchmarks. Self-Taught Evaluators also reports useful synthetic-training gains, alongside residual order sensitivity and uneven gains across tasks. Its training loop’s lack of new preference labels does not remove human influence from the seed, prompts or benchmarks. [Proxy versus downstream quality](claims.md#c74) · [Synthetic judge training](claims.md#c75).

Statistical methods can make judge use more defensible without choosing the right reference for us. The Alternative Annotator Test compares against sampled human judgments under an explicit cost-benefit rule; imbalance and minority weighting can change the answer. Skewed Score models grading, item effects and uncertainty in richer ways, but its worked examples are simulated. Neither a statistical pass nor a modeled adjustment establishes external truth or independent human research review. [Task-specific substitution](claims.md#c76) · [Statistical grading models](claims.md#c77).

### Monitoring, strategic behavior and mechanistic evidence

Control and monitoring studies provide system-level comparisons. Greenblatt et al. test monitoring, routing and editing protocols under a constructed intentional-subversion threat model, finding conditional safety/usefulness improvements over simpler protocols. Their safety measure is a simulated scenario probability, and their high-quality auditing labor is itself modeled. Monitorability research finds useful effects from reasoning-trace access and monitor compute, alongside observation-scope, label, obfuscation and endpoint limits. These are positive empirical and design contributions; they do not certify deployed safety, faithful reasoning or harm prevented. [Control protocols](claims.md#c30) · [Monitoring evidence](claims.md#c31).

Mechanistic validation has affirmative results as well as metric failures. Karvonen’s chess/Othello experiments recover formally specified board properties and distinguish trained from random models on board reconstruction. Initial-position masking and strategy-label controls also expose shortcuts. Cunningham’s pinned arXiv edition reports counterfactual IOI patching that changes outputs with fewer directions or smaller edits than tested baselines. Makelov’s task-reference dictionaries expose gaps in sparse control, particularly for full-distribution SAEs; shared IOI lineage limits task diversity. Braun improves sparsity/output-fidelity tradeoffs while showing that output matching can leave downstream activations and feature directions divergent; downstream reconstruction mitigates that problem. These studies support distinct tests of recovery, causal control and computational fidelity. They do not supply one universal validity score. [Structure recovery](claims.md#c48) · [Causal editing and controls](claims.md#c49) · [Output versus pathway fidelity](claims.md#c50).

Circuit tracing adds selected interventions on original-model outputs, including an intermediate-state swap that changes a capital-city answer as predicted. The companion methods and biology articles are one research family. Frozen attention, reconstruction error, feature selection and intervention-strength choices bound their validation; a direct effect that follows from the constrained setup is different from predicting downstream effects. Paulo’s feature-description study further separates activation-context and output-effect scoring, while Lan’s filtered cross-model similarities provide structural evidence without demonstrating transfer of causal control. Those two inspected editions remain explicitly related to their canonical records. [Circuit hypotheses and interventions](claims.md#c51) · [Description endpoints](claims.md#c52) · [Cross-model structure](claims.md#c53).

Templeton and Kamath’s selected features and interventions remain complementary evidence. Heap’s aggregate-metric controls limit particular metrics, rather than all interpretability methods; its named Control performs worse, and token-complexity and loss-recovery results differ. Heap was not reread in the latest round. Together these sources favor testing the intended use with positive task references, negative controls and alternative explanations before treating interpretable features as reliable safeguards. [Earlier mechanistic evidence and scope](claims.md#c13).

Evaluation awareness needs similarly separate endpoints. Nguyen and Chaudhary detect selected prompt-context differences in internal representations; this is not itself observed deception or a universal behavioral scaling law. Nguyen’s stronger simple suffix and unrelated-feature controls limit an awareness-specific steering account. Hua’s engineered organism gives positive controlled intervention evidence, with explicit cues and known-target limitations. Li’s instructed sandbagging uses conditional approval on a noisy success subset. SHADE-Arena adds longer agent tasks and held-out monitor-training gains, but its filtering, observation access and detection timing define a different target from real-time prevention. [Context discrimination](claims.md#c70) · [Steering and alternatives](claims.md#c71) · [Conditional concealment](claims.md#c72) · [Agent-task monitoring](claims.md#c73).

Loss-of-control studies address a different question. Sheshadri et al.'s broader alignment-faking study reports a compliance gap in five of 25 models under its initial elicitation and identifies goal preservation as a consistent explanation for one. Absence under that elicitation does not establish incapability. Taylor et al.'s auditing game deliberately introduces sandbagging proxies. Kirk et al. distinguishes spontaneous behavior from continuations induced by the experimental setup and reports no confirmed spontaneous sabotage; continuation behavior differs across models. Conflicting internal awareness/refusal percentages remain unresolved and are not quoted here. Monitoring studies show useful signals under tested conditions. Alert usefulness in deployment also depends on sensitivity, false positives, base rates and whether access to the signal is preserved. [Templeton](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/) · [Kamath](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attention-qk/index.html) · [Heap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17727) · [Sheshadri](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-1771) · [Taylor](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07810v1) · [Kirk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24618)

### Fairness and public input

Fairness and public input add an outcome-definition question. Collective Constitutional AI reports lower BBQ bias across nine tested dimensions after a public-derived constitution, alongside lower OpinionQA representativeness across five U.S. ideology groups. This is evidence of changed behavior under two metrics, with a screened U.S. sample and researcher-mediated principles; it does not establish universal consent or direct principle adherence. Swamy’s minimax method constructively handles intransitive preferences under its assumptions without settling whose preferences should count. TrustLLM treats subjective-choice neutrality/refusal as one fairness endpoint; DecodingTrust’s salary-prediction gaps measure another. A refusal can help one score and worsen another. Keep each construct, population and comparator explicit. [Public-input result and counterevidence](claims.md#c57) · [Minimax preferences](claims.md#c58) · [Distinct fairness constructs](claims.md#c59).

## 4. Governance documents show who says what should happen, with different legal force and decision scope

### Legal force and threshold choices

The inspected governance sources document a recurring structure in frontier frameworks: screen capabilities, evaluate specified risks, identify controls, and condition activities on a decision. It does not establish common thresholds. The Seoul commitments are voluntary. EU statutory duties, the voluntary GPAI Code, nonbinding Commission guidelines, and the statutory training-summary obligation have different authority. NIST and ISO methods or standards can inform controls and assurance without independently imposing a statutory duty. Provider policies can use compute as a screening proxy, capability criteria or scenario-specific residual-risk reasoning. A framework score such as SaferAI's measures documentary performance against that scoring rubric. The OECD counted 20 voluntary submissions to the Hiroshima reporting framework between February and June 2025. Their accounts show what participants reported, with safeguard adoption beyond the respondents and independent verification still open. [First-round reporting](claims.md#c17). [Seoul text](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024) · [EU GPAI Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai) · [NIST GenAI Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) · [Stelling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)

A fresh official-source check closes the earlier New York access/effective-date gap: the codified Article 44-B index displays the April 3, 2026 revision and January 1, 2027 effectiveness, supported by the signed amendment’s timing provisions. This narrow confirmation leaves actual implementation and application-specific legal questions separate. [Prospective statutory force](claims.md#c17).

Threshold design needs a stated purpose. Heim and the California report give compute a proposed early-screening role, with capability, context, post-training and safeguards affecting risk. Sastry identifies concrete infrastructure visibility/access levers alongside surveillance, concentration and evasion costs. The threshold survey captures selected expert preferences and disagreement; optional questions and competing definitions do not establish representative consensus or a calibrated risk boundary. Assessment, external scrutiny, deployment conditions and monitoring are proposed routes from a trigger to action, with measurement and institutional capacity still needed. [Early scrutiny](claims.md#c80) · [Compute levers](claims.md#c81) · [Survey limits](claims.md#c82) · [Adaptive governance](claims.md#c83).

NIST’s two historical misuse-risk drafts supply explicit risk-baseline, evaluation, safeguard, decision and feedback practices. Their draft-specific scope and voluntary character distinguish them from adopted statutory duties or measured implementation. California’s official signing material confirms SB 53’s enactment, while direct statutory-text access remains blocked in this round; inherited detailed deadlines and present consolidated application remain qualified. [Draft process design](claims.md#c100) · [Instrument/source status](claims.md#c17).

### Provider conditions and assurance arguments

Compare dates and provisions within issuers first. DeepMind moved its cyber weight-security recommendation from SL2 to SL2+ while retaining its rationale about defensive and societal adaptation. OpenAI v2 separates a conditional halt upon attained Critical capability from development safeguards for attained or forecast Critical capability. The main dataset already captures that distinction. Later internal-use or containment statements should remain separate observations from the numbered framework. Microsoft’s February 2026 edition changed screening scope and timing relative to its February 2025 edition while retaining materially similar offensive cyber threshold descriptions. xAI’s December 2025 framework reports relying on basic refusal for its cyber assessment and contemplates tiered access. It does not include the underlying third-party cyber evidence needed to verify that assessment. These documentary differences are reviewable when the exact versions, scope and modality travel with each claim. DeepMind v3.1 footnote 4 concerns omission of a critical-capability assessment under specified conditions, not exemption from all governance. Current legal force and policy currency must be checked separately from these historical observations. Goemans et al.'s inability-oriented safety case asks whether a defined threat actor can elicit a dangerous capability. Habli et al.'s safety-case proposal takes a broader view of the system and organizational context. Mitchell et al.'s model cards support disclosure, and their discussion calls for complementary scrutiny. These artifacts can be used together by giving each claim an explicit test, counterevidence, decision owner and review trigger. The corpus does not establish that safety cases are the only valid governance method. [Goemans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08088v1) · [Habli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11705) · [Mitchell](https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287596)

### Audit and reporting institutions

Government records document activity at different stages. The UK AI Safety Institute reported joint testing after its April 2024 agreement with the US and 16 models evaluated across its whole first year. The institute expressly withheld safety endorsement. The US Bureau of Industry and Security reported collecting information through a mandatory January 2024 survey of selected developers. Its separate September proposal for quarterly reporting was recorded as withdrawn in December 2025. [Government testing](claims.md#c104) · [Survey and reporting proposal](claims.md#c105).

Audit institutions connect these methods to decisions. Charnock and Casper distinguish access to interfaces, internals, process information, time and resources. Raji's external-audit analysis adds selection, payment, standards, follow-up and disclosure arrangements; limited public disclosure with fuller regulator access is one proposed design. Safe-harbor work distinguishes legal protection from technical account/access protection. These are conditional enabling mechanisms, with costs, security and exclusion trade-offs, rather than an established effect of giving every evaluator more information. Two apparently swapped audit-program examples in an appendix are omitted while the separately supported institutional analysis remains usable. [Access conditions](claims.md#c38) · [Institutional design](claims.md#c39) · [Legal and technical protection](claims.md#c40).

Internal and external review can be complementary. The proposed SMACTR lifecycle connects artifacts, testing and post-audit action, while acknowledging organizational interests and outside checks; its worked Company X examples are hypothetical. Datasheets similarly proposes a reflective lifecycle-documentation workflow, with practical communication and selection uses. Automatically populated metadata does not perform that reflective process or demonstrate representativeness. Anthropic’s October 2024 update reports that its first internal adherence review identified procedural departures and a need to improve compliance tracking. Evidence of effective follow-up and independent assurance remains open. [Lifecycle audit complementarity](claims.md#c41) · [Dataset documentation](claims.md#c44).

The deferred audit papers make the proposed mechanisms more specific. Longpre’s final paper connects standardized flaw reports, conduct-based protections, access and coordination, while leaving remediation effects unmeasured. Its “third-party” excludes a direct contractual relationship; Mökander and Homewood use the term for external collaborative or commissioned review. Mökander’s blueprint is informed by a systematised review and stakeholder consultation, but is not an implemented complete audit. It links governance, model and application evidence rather than assuming a model score settles context-specific fairness. Homewood distinguishes reviewing adherence to a framework from reviewing its adequacy; its mock control/maturity tables are constructed examples. [Access and reporting](claims.md#c38) · [Layered audit feedback](claims.md#c54) · [Compliance versus adequacy](claims.md#c55).

Implementation evidence is more informative when its stage is explicit. Historical SBA, EAC and EXIM plans report organizational or policy acts alongside prospective risk procedures; none supplies an independently checked effectiveness result. April 2025 M-25-21 specifies a qualified federal-use testing/review/acceptance/monitoring pathway; its nondeveloper reviewer is within the agency. It is a historical comparator, not a frontier-provider rule or current-law certification. The pinned federal inventory supplies a concrete disclosure artifact: a documentary recount of its v2 file matches the README’s 2,133 records, 41 agencies and 351 declared rights/safety cases. These are administrative records across lifecycle stages, not a count of deployed systems or an observed compliance rate. [Agency plans](claims.md#c60) · [Mandated pathway](claims.md#c61) · [Inventory snapshot and file selection](claims.md#c62).

Provider evidence also differs by object. FMTI 2025 scores disclosed information, using issuer sources and company responses; changed indicators and reporting methods prevent a controlled annual safety trend. Taken together, the sources support task transfer and multi-step assistance in particular configurations. Access conditions and observed autonomy vary substantially. ARTEMIS gives a descriptive performance comparison in one authorized setting. Its scaffold drew on a previously observed human attack graph, and the study lacks a matched AI-assistance counterfactual. Harm mapping remains unresolved: action, compromise, credential exposure, persistence and damage are separate outcomes. Hardened targets and human intervention provide attributed examples of pathways being interrupted. They do not establish the average offset or durability of defenses under adaptation. ARTEMIS and Souly bound generalization to defended environments, and the source-level review leads record that distinction. The supplied cyber corrections are integrated in the dataset, retaining earlier assessments and adding dated review assessments; ARTEMIS and Souly now bound defense-persistence generalization.

Contextual and noncyber work reinforces the outcome boundary. Salvi et al.'s randomized ten-minute debates found higher odds of an increase in self-reported agreement for personalized GPT-4 versus the unpersonalized human baseline (OR 1.812, 95% CI 1.260–2.607). The corrected direct contrast with unpersonalized GPT-4 is OR 1.487 (95% CI 0.971–2.276, p=0.0678). The 2026 correction identifies a reference-category calculation error in the earlier p=0.04. The corrected interval leaves the added personalization effect uncertain. A fixed-threshold odds conversion from the supplement should be omitted from general claims about the ordinal outcome. Hackenburg and Margetts found persuasive effects from generated messages without a detectable added microtargeting benefit in their experiment. These different interventions and outcomes leave effects in actual campaigns open. Sociotechnical evaluation frameworks distinguish model, system and impact levels, while incident registries and media-based monitors have reporting and denominator limits. ## How this could change

The new sources support a constructive, conditional account of evaluation: specified methods can improve comparison, supervision, monitoring and documentation, while institutional design affects whether those findings can inform a decision. This adds a missing argument to a synthesis centered mainly on inference limits. A publication could lead with those enabling conditions, or with the unresolved barriers to using them at frontier scale. That interpretive choice remains the maintainer's; the research should expose both the gains and the strongest alternative explanations.

Before the November 20 targeted intake, four bounded rounds logged 142 events for 106 keys. The fourth added 38 events across 31 keys and resolved 30 previously unresolved roles.
