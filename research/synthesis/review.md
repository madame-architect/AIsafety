## 1. A benchmark result describes performance under an evaluation configuration

The technical corpus contains capability tests, adversarial safety tests, training and mitigation experiments, evaluator studies, and proposed evaluation methods. They yield different evidence. Phuong et al. v2 reports several early-warning evaluations. Table 23 and Appendix F.5 concern a toy local safety-filter modification task in self-reasoning: the Pro-CoT easy/easy condition scores 0.50 against a 0.70 passing criterion. This is distinct from the self-proliferation tasks. Table 11 uses modeled one-sided 97.5% upper bounds for milestone-based tasks, conditional on the stated priors and milestone assumptions. These results characterize that protocol and model configuration. Folkerts et al. uses multi-step enterprise and industrial-control ranges with explicit inference budgets. OSWorld, TheAgentCompany and τ-bench expand the scope to tool use, longer workflows and repeated-trial reliability, respectively. Each adds a missing dimension. Any comparison requires matching model version, harness, tools and permissions, retries, time or tokens, safeguards, judge and task distribution. Even a real application can be part of a controlled environment. [Phuong v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793v2) · [Folkerts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11214) · [OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972) · [TheAgentCompany](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161) · [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)

The differences can reverse apparent rankings. The RE-Bench authors report the best tested agents ahead at two hours and human experts ahead at longer budgets. Murray et al.'s experts disagreed on whether strong CTF performance removed a practical bottleneck or left the harder task of connecting capabilities in a live workflow. Their elicited conditional probability change is expert judgment. WMDP evaluates hazardous knowledge and unlearning on a proxy, while Joshi et al. and Liu et al. examine leakage under changed prompts and the inadequacy of some unlearning metrics. Together these studies support explicit transfer tests and boundary conditions before translating proxy results into an operational threshold. [RE-Bench](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wijk25a.html) · [Murray v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04299v2) · [WMDP](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24bc.html) · [Joshi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15477) · [Liu](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-00985-0)

## 2. Safeguard results depend on the adversary, control and outcome definition

Anil et al. found that several fine-tuning methods lowered baseline many-shot jailbreak success while leaving the growth in success with more examples largely intact. Ackerman and Panickssery report flatter slopes under their adversarial fine-tuning and input sanitization on Llama 3.1 8B, under the tested context limit. Some paired judgments also favored untuned answers on benign long conversations. Different models and training examples leave the disagreement about generality open. Sharma et al.'s Constitutional Classifiers and subsequent adaptive jailbreak studies use different defense generations, success criteria and feedback. Their results cannot be pooled as a single bypass rate. [Anil §§5.1–5.2](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ea456e232efb72d261715e33ce25f208-Paper-Conference.pdf) · [Ackerman and Panickssery v3 §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09604v3) · [Sharma](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18837)

The security literature covers distinct control surfaces. Instruction hierarchy and prompt-injection defenses address conflicting instructions. Open-weight tamper resistance addresses model modification. Pretraining poisoning and backdoor studies concern different attacker access, objectives and persistence. A refusal rate cannot establish system authorization or egress enforcement, and a successful tamper-resistance result remains bounded by the tested modification budget. NIST's adversarial ML taxonomy provides useful language for comparing threats, and supplies no blanket assurance for the controls it describes. [Wallace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208) · [Tamirisa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00761) · [Souly on poisoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07192) · [NIST AI 100-2e2025](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-2e2025)

Evidence trail: [many-shot defenses](claims.md#c05), [classifier criteria](claims.md#c06), [threat/control surfaces](claims.md#c07). The supplied citation correction identifies Liu’s intended USENIX paper while retaining its existing key; StruQ is a separate work.

## 3. Evaluation integrity is itself an empirical question

The alignment and oversight papers offer ways to improve preference data, reward learning, debate, and model grading. HelpSteer2 and its preference extension share data lineage. Better RewardBench performance can support a narrow judge comparison. Wang et al.'s positional-bias study and Xu et al.'s leakage study address distinct threats to relying on a judge or leaderboard. Xu's string matching is a proxy for possible exposure, and the printed score definition and threshold need reconciliation with the released code before using the rates. Khan et al. and Kenton et al. find debate can help under specific information asymmetries and judge setups. In Kenton's extractive QA experiment, judges with the full passage and direct QA outperformed debate across the tested extractive judge configurations. Kenton v2 also distinguishes overall accuracy from error detection: open consultancy can be more accurate overall while open debate better identifies protagonist errors. Khan v4 Appendix A.6 reports a quote-only ablation outperforming quotes plus arguments; evidence selection and verification remain an alternative explanation for some benefit. These are inference-time findings under the tested information access and judge setups, rather than proof of a general training method. The results favor validation against humans, held-out distributions and simpler controls for each intended use. [HelpSteer2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08673) · [HelpSteer2-Preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01257) · [Wang on judge bias](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.511/) · [Xu on leakage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18824) · [Khan v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06782v4) · [Kenton v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04622v2)

Interpretability and loss-of-control papers address complementary evidence. Templeton et al. and Kamath et al. identify interpretable features or selected attention computations and test interventions. Heap et al. show that some aggregate SAE metrics can score randomized-model variants comparably; their named Control performs worse. Token-complexity and loss-recovery results differ, so the result limits particular validation metrics rather than establishing that all interpretability measures fail. Sheshadri et al.'s broader alignment-faking study reports a compliance gap in five of 25 models under its initial elicitation and identifies goal preservation as a consistent explanation for one. Absence under that elicitation does not establish incapability. Taylor et al.'s auditing game deliberately introduces sandbagging proxies. Kirk et al. distinguishes spontaneous behavior from continuations induced by the experimental setup and reports no confirmed spontaneous sabotage; continuation behavior differs across models. Conflicting internal awareness/refusal percentages remain unresolved and are not quoted here. Monitoring studies show useful signals under tested conditions. Alert usefulness in deployment also depends on sensitivity, false positives, base rates and whether access to the signal is preserved. [Templeton](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/) · [Kamath](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attention-qk/index.html) · [Heap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17727) · [Sheshadri](https://doi.org/10.52202/085713-1771) · [Taylor](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07810v1) · [Kirk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24618)

Evidence trail: [data lineage](claims.md#c08), [judge bias](claims.md#c09), [leakage](claims.md#c10), [debate and alternatives](claims.md#c11), [mechanistic evidence](claims.md#c13), [elicited versus spontaneous behavior](claims.md#c14).

## 4. Governance documents show who says what should happen, with different legal force and decision scope

The inspected governance sources document a recurring structure in frontier frameworks: screen capabilities, evaluate specified risks, identify controls, and condition activities on a decision. It does not establish common thresholds. The Seoul commitments are voluntary. EU statutory duties, the voluntary GPAI Code, nonbinding Commission guidelines, and the statutory training-summary obligation have different authority. NIST and ISO methods or standards can inform controls and assurance without independently imposing a statutory duty. Provider policies can use compute as a screening proxy, capability criteria or scenario-specific residual-risk reasoning. A framework score such as SaferAI's measures documentary performance against that scoring rubric. The OECD Hiroshima pilot records participant disclosures. Neither yields a control effectiveness rate. [Seoul text](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024) · [EU GPAI Code](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai) · [NIST GenAI Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1) · [Stelling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)

Compare dates and provisions within issuers first. DeepMind moved its cyber weight-security recommendation from SL2 to SL2+ while retaining its rationale about defensive and societal adaptation. OpenAI v2 separates a conditional halt upon attained Critical capability from development safeguards for attained or forecast Critical capability. The main dataset already captures that distinction. Later internal-use or containment statements should remain separate observations from the numbered framework. Microsoft’s February 2026 edition changed screening scope and timing relative to its February 2025 edition while retaining materially similar offensive cyber threshold descriptions. xAI’s December 2025 framework reports relying on basic refusal for its cyber assessment and contemplates tiered access. It does not include the underlying third-party cyber evidence needed to verify that assessment. These documentary differences are reviewable when the exact versions, scope and modality travel with each claim. DeepMind v3.1 footnote 4 concerns omission of a critical-capability assessment under specified conditions, not exemption from all governance. Current legal force and policy currency must be checked separately from these historical observations. [DeepMind v3.0](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework/frontier-safety-framework_3.pdf) · [DeepMind v3.1](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework/frontier-safety-framework_3-1.pdf) · [OpenAI v2 Table 1, printed pp. 5–6, and §§4.3–4.4](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf) · [Microsoft 2026](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/Frontier-Governance-Framework-Feb-2026.pdf) · [xAI December 2025](https://data.x.ai/2025-12-31-xai-frontier-artificial-intelligence-framework.pdf)

Goemans et al.'s inability-oriented safety case asks whether a defined threat actor can elicit a dangerous capability. Habli et al.'s safety-case proposal takes a broader view of the system and organizational context. Mitchell et al.'s model cards support disclosure, and their discussion calls for complementary scrutiny. These artifacts can be used together by giving each claim an explicit test, counterevidence, decision owner and review trigger. The corpus does not establish that safety cases are the only valid governance method. [Goemans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08088v1) · [Habli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11705) · [Mitchell](https://doi.org/10.1145/3287560.3287596)

[ARTEMIS v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09882v2) · [ICLR final](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=Us00XndbVi&name=pdf) · [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/ai-augmented-threat-actor-accesses-fortigate-devices-at-scale/) · [GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai) · [AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) · [Souly v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38415v1) · [Barrett v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08864v2) · [Murray v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04299v2)

Taken together, the sources support task transfer and multi-step assistance in particular configurations. Access conditions and observed autonomy vary substantially. ARTEMIS gives a descriptive performance comparison in one authorized setting. Its scaffold drew on a previously observed human attack graph, and the study lacks a matched AI-assistance counterfactual. Harm mapping remains unresolved: action, compromise, credential exposure, persistence and damage are separate outcomes. Hardened targets and human intervention provide attributed examples of pathways being interrupted. They do not establish the average offset or durability of defenses under adaptation. ARTEMIS and Souly bound generalization to defended environments, and the source-level review leads record that distinction. The supplied cyber corrections are integrated in the dataset, retaining earlier assessments and adding dated review assessments; ARTEMIS and Souly now bound defense-persistence generalization.

Contextual and noncyber work reinforces the outcome boundary. Salvi et al.'s randomized ten-minute debates found higher odds of an increase in self-reported agreement for personalized GPT-4 versus the unpersonalized human baseline (OR 1.812, 95% CI 1.260–2.607). The corrected direct contrast with unpersonalized GPT-4 is OR 1.487 (95% CI 0.971–2.276, p=0.0678). The 2026 correction identifies a reference-category calculation error in the earlier p=0.04. The corrected interval leaves the added personalization effect uncertain. A fixed-threshold odds conversion from the supplement should be omitted from general claims about the ordinal outcome. Hackenburg and Margetts found persuasive effects from generated messages without a detectable added microtargeting benefit in their experiment. These different interventions and outcomes leave effects in actual campaigns open. Sociotechnical evaluation frameworks distinguish model, system and impact levels, while incident registries and media-based monitors have reporting and denominator limits. [Salvi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02194-6) · [2026 Author Correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02588-0) · [Hackenburg and Margetts](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2403116121) · [Weidinger](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11986) · [OECD incident monitor](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents)

## How this could change

A more affirmative interpretation could emphasize demonstrated task performance, successful interventions, useful oversight contrasts and real remediation chains. A more skeptical interpretation could emphasize transfer gaps, adaptive adversaries, uncertain judges and discretionary governance. The present evidence supports both targeted progress and substantial inference limits. Which argument should lead is a maintainer decision; the corpus should test the thesis rather than supply decoration for a predetermined one.

The full-corpus objective remains open: 181 records do not carry inherited full-text labels, and even a full-text label does not close version, figure, appendix, claim or replication gaps. Complete claim-critical sources first, preserve contrary results and related-study dependencies, and continue the remaining record review. A completed read of an inherited collection would still not reconstruct systematic search or independent human coding.

For a bounded optional cyber publication, retain the existing plan of two decision/control cases and one defender comparator. Freeze the question and actual query log before a new search round; code capability, commitment, implementation and effect separately; seek a counterexample for each case; and stop at an auditable case matrix for maintainer review. This is a future research protocol, not a claim that this integration completed that round. Final thesis, venue and publication remain undecided.
